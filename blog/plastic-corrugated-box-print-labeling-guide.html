Printing and Labeling Plastic Corrugated Boxes: A Spec Guide

Plasticcorrugatedbox Editorial • October 2026

Why Marking Twin-Wall PP Is Different

Plastic corrugated boxes solve the durability and hygiene problems of fiberboard, but they introduce a surface-marking challenge procurement teams rarely anticipate. Polypropylene is a low-surface-energy plastic, which means standard inks and label adhesives that bond easily to paper or cardboard can peel, smear, or flake off PP without proper preparation. In a closed-loop reusable program, a box might cycle through dozens of trips, washdowns, and outdoor yard storage, so any branding, routing codes, or regulatory markings must survive the full service life. Getting the print and label spec right up front prevents unreadable labels and compliance gaps down the line.

Surface Treatment: The Foundation of Adhesion

Because untreated PP resists ink and adhesive, the surface usually needs activation. The two common methods are corona treatment and flame treatment, both of which raise the surface energy so inks and adhesives can wet out and bond. For high-volume branded fleets, corona treatment applied during manufacturing produces the most consistent results. Buyers specifying custom boxes should confirm whether the supplier pre-treats the printing face, since a box that prints beautifully on day one but fails after a wash cycle was almost certainly under-treated.

When you order custom plastic corrugated boxes from Elipacko, surface treatment and print requirements can be specified at the manufacturing stage, so adhesion is engineered in rather than bolted on afterward. That matters most for boxes carrying permanent branding or regulatory text that must stay legible across the whole reuse program.

Ink and Printing Method Options

  • Screen printing: Durable, opaque, and well-suited to twin-wall PP; ideal for logos and large text that must survive abrasion and washing.
  • Flexographic printing: Economical for high volumes and multi-color runs on sheet before conversion.
  • Digital/UV printing: Flexible for short runs and variable data; UV-cured inks bond well to treated PP.
  • Hot stamping: Clean single-color marks for part numbers and simple branding.

For regulatory or routing information that changes per shipment, printed-in-place text is impractical — that is where labels come in.

Label Selection for Reusable PP Boxes

Labels on reusable boxes fall into two camps: permanent and swappable. Permanent labels carry fixed data such as owner identification, tare weight, or compliance marks and should use high-tack acrylic adhesives rated for low-surface-energy plastics and wide temperature ranges. Swappable labels carry per-trip routing and should sit in a label holder or use a clean-release adhesive so they strip off without residue. For wet or cold-chain environments, specify synthetic facestock rather than paper, since paper labels disintegrate under moisture and washdown.

A smart spec pairs a molded or printed label window on the box face with a weather-resistant insert, keeping per-trip labels protected while the box body carries permanent branding. This is straightforward to design into a custom box but nearly impossible to retrofit, which is another reason to settle marking requirements before the mold or tooling is finalized.

Durability Testing You Should Request

Before committing to a fleet, ask the supplier for adhesion and abrasion data under your real conditions: cross-hatch tape adhesion after wash cycles, label peel strength at cold-chain temperatures, and ink rub resistance after UV exposure. A supplier confident in its surface treatment and ink system will provide these readily. If marking durability data is unavailable, assume the markings are not validated for reuse.

The Bottom Line

On twin-wall PP, legible lasting marks depend on surface treatment, the right ink system, and synthetic labels with adhesives rated for low-energy plastics.
